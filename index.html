career objective

I want to build the hardware that lets systems see and talk at microwave frequencies — radar front-ends, test instruments, and the mixed-signal boards that connect them to software.

Most of what I've learned has come from carrying a design the whole way: block diagram, simulation in ADS, schematic and stackup in KiCad, layout, and then the slow part — bring-up and measurement in the lab. After graduating I want to join an RF/microwave hardware team (radar, test & measurement, or wireless) where I can keep doing that full loop on harder problems and learn from engineers who have shipped real products.

Longer term I'd like to grow toward graduate-level depth in electromagnetics and microwave circuit design and lead the design of measurement systems — like the VNA and SAR imaging scanner I work on now — that make high-frequency hardware cheaper and easier to test.

senior design project
Radar · 24–40 GHzPCB / KiCadsdmay27-11 · Team Leader
MIMO FMCW Radar — 24 to 40 GHz
EE 4910/4920 senior design, team sdmay27-11 (seven students). Client and faculty advisor: Dr. Mohammed Tayeb Al Qaseer.
→
System block diagram AD3551R fast-sweep DAC — 3D AD3551R board — top sheet AD3551R + AD8065 buffer Simplified 0–10 V sweeper — 3D Simplified sweeper — layout Simplified sweeper — schematic
Description

A homodyne MIMO FMCW radar that sweeps the full 24–40 GHz band. A DAC ramp tunes a wideband VCO to make a linear chirp, which is up-converted, sent out through a waveguide antenna, and mixed with the returning echo. The beat frequency between the two chirps gives range; sweeping 16 GHz gives a range resolution of ΔR = c/2B ≈ 9.4 mm.

My hardware on the project is the sweep source — the DAC boards that generate the VCO tuning ramp:

  • AD3551R fast-sweep DAC: hierarchical KiCad design with SPI over shielded RJ45, per-line TXB0101 3.3 V→1.8 V translators, an AD8065 transimpedance output buffer, 5 V switcher + 1.8 V LDO, and jumpers to bring up each rail one at a time
  • Simplified sweeper: after reviewing risk, I replaced the current-output DAC with a voltage-output DAC driven by a small STM32 over SPI, amplified ×3 by a non-inverting stage for a 0–10 V tuning sweep — a minimum viable board that is far easier to bring up
My role — Team Leader
  • Came up with the project with Dr. Tayeb over the summer and got the team started at the beginning of the semester
  • Defined the system block diagram and selected the main IC for each block
  • Split the radar into subsystems with owners (RF boards, VCO/PLL, antenna, signal processing, FPGA), set weekly plans, and tracked status in the weekly reports
  • Led the shift to a minimum-viable-product plan to cut risk; designed both DAC boards
Skills & knowledge gained
  • Leading a seven-person team: delegating subsystems, keeping work moving, and reporting progress to a client
  • Scoping down to a minimum viable product when the first design is more than the project needs
  • Precision DAC layout: extended QFN pads, short low-capacitance output traces, level translation and staged power bring-up
Big-picture contribution

Draft — please check: Wideband mm-wave radars are expensive and closed. This project gives the lab an open, millimeter-resolution 24–40 GHz radar built from off-the-shelf parts that future teams can extend to more channels and MIMO imaging.

HUBRIS VNA — Vector Network Analyzer from Scratch
A 2-port VNA designed layer by layer: a 2.3–2.8 GHz VCO, directional couplers, diode mixers, a 6-layer STM32H7 motherboard, and Python calibration and material-characterization software. Started in EE 514 (Fall 2025) and continued as an EE 4900 independent study with Prof. Nathan Neihart (Spring 2026). Long-term goal: 0.5–10 GHz with >130 dB dynamic range.
→
2.3–2.8 GHz VCO board — exploded from the fab Gerbers
VNA motherboard — KiCad 3D Top view: MCU, Ethernet, downconverters
System block diagram 6-layer stackup 2.3–2.8 GHz VCO layout VCO output vs. tuning VCO test board — KiCad 3D Stepped VCO tune sweep Rat-race coupler layout Rat-race S-parameters (ADS) Rat-race in CST Edge-coupled coupler in CST Balanced 180° diode mixer Mixer IF output VNA plugin in the scanner GUI
Description
  • Signal chain: VCO → directional coupler → DUT and reference downconverters → balanced 180° diode mixer (≈6 dB conversion loss) → anti-alias filter → balun → dual AD9238 ADC at 20 MSPS → STM32H7 → Ethernet → PC, where an FFT recovers magnitude and phase
  • VCO: modified Colpitts, common-base, biased at 5 mA for minimum noise figure; two SMV1247 varactors (0.7–7 pF) tune 2.3–2.8 GHz at about 0 dBm, with simulated phase noise of −165 to −173 dBc/Hz at 1 MHz
  • Couplers: branch-line, rat-race (Zring = 70.7 Ω) and modified rat-race; a 10 dB edge-coupled design (Z0e = 69.4 Ω, Z0o = 36.1 Ω) hit 10 ± 0.5 dB coupling but only ≈10 dB directivity, so I derived the even/odd phase-velocity limit and wrote a Kirschning–Jansen + SciPy optimizer for gap and length
  • Motherboard: 6-layer stackup from the STM32H7 guidelines to fan out every ADC line; USB-C, Ethernet and SWD; the tune voltage comes from my STM32F1 + DAC stepped-sweep board and power from my 20 V / 60 W USB-C PD board
  • Software: SOLT one-port calibration (3-term error model, ideal or measured standards, coax de-embedding), NRW extraction of complex εr and μr, and a threaded vna_plugin.py that runs SOL calibration at scan start and writes raw, corrected and material data per point to the lab scanner's HDF5 files
My role

Sole designer across every layer of the instrument — RF circuits in ADS and CST, the motherboard in KiCad, firmware protocol, calibration math, and the scanner integration.

Skills & knowledge gained
  • Even/odd-mode coupler analysis and why microstrip limits directivity
  • Oscillator design against the Barkhausen criterion and varactor tuning
  • VNA error models, SOLT calibration and de-embedding
  • Knowing when full-wave simulation disagrees with circuit models — and why (port impedance, conductor loss)
  • Mixed-signal layout for high-speed ADCs
Resources used

Keysight ADS, CST Microwave Studio, KiCad 9, Python (NumPy, SciPy, scikit-rf); R. K. Mongia's RF and Microwave Coupled-Line Circuits, Pozar's Microwave Engineering, Kirschning & Jansen coupled-line models, Skyworks, Analog Devices and ST datasheets; guidance from Prof. Nathan Neihart.

Next steps
  • Fabricate and measure the VCO and coupler boards
  • Lange coupler or capacitive compensation for better directivity
  • End-to-end validation of the full chain, then an FPGA back end for N-port measurements
  • ADAR2001/2004 front ends to reach Ka-band

Phased-Array FMCW Radar — Up/Down Converter
Four-channel Ka-band FMCW radar built around an ADAR2001/ADAR2004 up/down-converter board, with a VCO sweep board and a USB-C power delivery board. With Justin Adrowski and Durotimi Johnson.
→
Up/down converter board — KiCad 3D Up/down converter — angled STM32F1 VCO sweep board — KiCad 3D USB-C PD power board — KiCad 3D Differential Vivaldi antenna Bistatic block diagram
Description
  • The up/down converter is the heart of the radar: an ADF4372 synthesizer (62.5 MHz–8 GHz and 8–16 GHz outputs) provides the LO, an ADAR2001 drives four differential RF outputs and an ADAR2004 receives four differential inputs
  • All eight RF pairs are meandered to equal length so the channels stay in phase for beam steering
  • LT3045 ultra-low-noise LDOs for the RF rails, RJ45 jacks for SPI and the differential IF out, USB-C and 20 V input
  • STM32F1 + DAC VCO sweep board (also reused as the VNA's stepped-frequency source), a 20 V USB-C Power Delivery board (60 W, 100 W with a few part swaps), and differential Vivaldi antennas for the array
My role

System architect and lead board designer on a three-person team — part selection, block diagrams, the up/down converter and power boards, and RF layout.

Skills & knowledge gained
  • Millimeter-wave part selection and link-budget trade-offs
  • Phase-matched differential routing, low-noise power, ESD protection and power sequencing
  • Splitting a large design into boards that can be brought up one at a time
Resources used

KiCad 9, STM32CubeIDE, Analog Devices / MACOM / Mini-Circuits datasheets and eval boards, EE 418 course material.
